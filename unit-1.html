Blog: The Pressure Freshman Feel and A Sense of Belonging

The phenomenon I plan to write my paper on is the pressure freshman feel to quickly find “their people” and a sense of belonging in a completely new environment. When I first got to Indiana University, I expected that adjusting to a new environment would take some time, but I was not ready for the pressure I would feel to figure out where I belong socially to come so quickly. Within my first few weeks, I was extremely homesick and missed the people and routines I was familiar with. At the same time, I found myself surrounded by thousands of new people, which made me feel like I was supposed to quickly find a group of friends and figure out where I fit in. Eventually, I started making friendships that felt more natural to me, and I have slowly begun to feel more and more comfortable. However, the process has made me think about why there seems to be so much pressure for freshmen to find their people so quickly.

One of my strongest patterns from my list of observations is acclimatizing to a new environment. Everything about college was new at first, including my surroundings, routines, classes, and social life. My homesickness made the transition even harder because I was comparing my new environment to the familiar life I had at home. On top of this, I got sick with Covid 19 weeks into school, so I was trapped away in my dorm for about a week, healing, and left with only my thoughts. I noticed that finding friends was connected to the feeling of comfort at college. As I slowly found people I connected with, Indiana began to feel less unfamiliar. This makes me feel like friendships can become more than just social relationships for new college students. On top of that, they can also give students a sense of stability and belonging when almost everything around them is changing.

However, I may be making assumptions as to why freshmen feel this pressure. I am assuming that other students feel the same pressure that I have felt, but everyone’s transition into college is different. I am also assuming that the pressure mainly comes from being in a new environment, but I have to remember that it is possible for things such as social media and popular culture to also shape what students think their college experience is supposed to look like. This leaves me with questions like: Why do freshmen feel like they need to find their people so quickly? How much does social media contribute to the expectation that college students should immediately have a close friend group? Why do people associate finding friends with finding where we belong? And what happens when a person’s college experience does not look like the version they see online or in popular culture?

One of the written popular culture artifacts I am considering is the University of Michigan's article “How to Find Your People at U-M.” The article discusses the pressure new college students can feel when they arrive on campus and wonder whether they will ever find their people. This connects closely to my own experience because I initially felt homesick and unsure of where I belonged at Indiana. The article challenges my current interpretation because it makes me consider that this pressure may not only come from being in a completely new environment. There may also be a larger cultural expectation that college is the time when students are supposed to quickly find their friend group and create long lasting relationships. If universities recognize that students worry about finding their people, it makes me wonder why this expectation is so strong in the first place. The article could therefore help me explore whether the pressure to belong comes from the transition to college, outside cultural expectations, or a combination of both.

While the pressure freshmen feel to quickly find their people may come from adjusting to a new environment, examining the way college friendships and the sense of belonging are portrayed in popular culture suggests that students may also feel pressured because they have been taught to expect college to be the place where they will instantly find their people.
